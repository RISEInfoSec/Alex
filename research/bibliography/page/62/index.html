  18. TITLE AUTONOMOUS SELF-REPAIRING ROBOTIC SWARMS AT THE LIMIT SUBTITLE Distributed Intelligence, Causal Fault Diagnosis, Reversible Repair, and Independent Requalification AUTHOR Feng Cheng-en (33) x STARLI Research Institute XXVII ENGLISH PUBLICATION DESCRIPTION What would count as genuine repair in a robot swarm? Mission continuation is not enough. A swarm may route around a damaged agent, reduce an unannounced requirement, sacrifice unmeasured capability, or transfer risk to its neighbors while appearing successful. Autonomous Self-Repairing Robotic Swarms at the Limit develops a falsifiable research architecture for distributed fault detection, causal diagnosis, risk isolation, reversible intervention, task redistribution, physical and morphological repair, and independent post-repair requalification. The central claim is deliberately bounded: when fault genealogy, capability envelopes, uncertainty-aware local evidence, reversible action, and independent qualification operate together, local repair should improve functional recovery without increasing latent failure. The monograph contains thirty linked research equations, eighty-two chapters with five substantial research sections each, and two hundred detailed Research Gates. Every gate specifies a question, registered hypothesis, minimum experiment, observables, strong baseline, falsifier, stopping rule, successor evidence package, and DOI anchors. The single-section / single-page architecture makes each research unit independently addressable and suitable for future human and AI reconstruction. The book separates repair from redundancy, rerouting, graceful degradation, replacement, and self-certification. It examines gradual and abrupt faults, common-cause failure, partial observability, calibration drift, network partition, Byzantine behavior, docking and connector fatigue, repair-induced damage, predictive maintenance, residual-life estimation, blind fault injection, withheld missions, human override, cybersecurity, vendor independence, retirement, and cross-generational handoff. Evidence is anchored in modular reconfigurable robotics, swarm fault diagnosis, self-healing collectives, resilient coordination, distributed metrology, and safe swarm design. The equations are transparent design heuristics rather than universal physical laws, and the integrated system remains a research hypothesis. Unknown states must trigger abstention; the repairer cannot be the sole verifier; moving thresholds invalidate success; irreversible harm and failed override are hard stops. This volume is written for robotics researchers, systems engineers, safety and verification specialists, autonomous-systems designers, AI researchers, and future successors who need not merely a vision of self-healing machines, but a testable path for deciding when a swarm has truly restored function - and when it must stop.
